## Pricing types

Each tariff has a `pricing_type` field that indicates how the price is calculated:

### Standard pricing (`pricing_type: "standard"`)

For tariffs with a selectable quantity (`count` contains `min` and `max`), the price is the sum of the costs of each quantity range.

The `discounts` field maps the **first unit number** in a range to its discount percentage. A discount applies from that unit up to the unit before the next threshold. Reaching a new threshold does not change the cost of earlier units.

For example, with a base unit price of **1** and `discounts: {"1": 0, "100": 10}`, ordering 150 units for one hour or one tariff period, before any user-specific discount, costs:

- Units 1–99: `99 × 1 = 99`
- Units 100–150: `51 × 1 × 0.90 = 45.90`
- Total: **144.90**

For hourly tariffs, multiply the amount by the number of hours. The server then applies the effective user discount and rounds the final total to two decimal places.

<Note>
  For tariffs with a selectable quantity, the API's `price` is the rounded total for `count.min`, for one hour or one tariff period, with applicable discounts already included. The base unit price used in the example is not exposed as a separate field in the tariff response.
</Note>

### Graduated pricing (`pricing_type: "graduated"`)

Price is a fixed amount based on quantity thresholds. The `graduated_prices` field contains an object where keys are quantity thresholds and values are prices **with all discounts already applied**.

For example, if `graduated_prices` is `{"0": 135, "101": 180, "201": 270}`:
- Ordering 50 units costs **135** per hour
- Ordering 150 units costs **180** per hour
- Ordering 250 units costs **270** per hour

For hourly tariffs, multiply the tier price by the number of hours.

<Note>
  The `graduated_prices` values already include your personal discount and any active promotions. The `discounts` field is not applicable for graduated pricing tariffs.
</Note>
